2. Executive Summary
DealSouk is a cross-platform mobile application (iOS and Android) that empowers Dubai
residents to compare real-time grocery prices across the major supermarket chains in the UAE.
Unlike static price-comparison websites or retailer-controlled apps, DealSouk is powered
entirely by crowd-sourced data: community members visit supermarkets, photograph price
labels, and submit prices in exchange for tokens. Those tokens accumulate toward redeemable
supermarket vouchers, creating a self-sustaining economy that keeps data current and
accurate.

Shoppers benefit from a live grocery cart that automatically surfaces the cheapest available
price for each item within their chosen vicinity, a map view of nearby participating supermarkets,
and the ability to request specific price data they need. Data Sourcers — community members
who physically collect prices — are motivated by a gamified token and leaderboard system that
makes contributing feel rewarding rather than transactional.

Version 1.0 targets the seven Emirates of the UAE with an initial focus on Dubai, launching
across the ten major supermarket chains that collectively represent over 80% of the emirate's
grocery market share.

3. Project Scope
3.1  In Scope — Version 1.0
• User registration and authentication (phone OTP, Apple Sign-In, Google Sign-In)
• Interactive map view displaying nearby supermarkets with live data availability indicators
• Product and price discovery: search, browse, and view crowd-sourced prices by
supermarket
• Grocery cart with automatic best-price surfacing per item within the user's configured
vicinity
• Item request system: shoppers can request specific price data from specific
supermarkets
• Data sourcing flow: photo capture, manual price entry, and submission for token rewards
• Token economy: requesters spend tokens; data sourcers earn tokens; voucher
redemption at threshold
• Gamification layer: leaderboard, badges, streaks, daily challenges, and level progression
• Trust and moderation: community flagging, auto-expiry, admin review queue
• Admin and data monitoring dashboard: price trends, product demand heatmaps, sourcer
activity
• Support for Arabic and English (RTL/LTR language switching)

3.2  Out of Scope — Version 1.0
• Wishlist / saved items lists
• Social media sharing or public user profiles
• Paid advertising or promotional placements for supermarkets within the app
• Integration with grocery delivery platforms (e.g., Carrefour Now, InstaShop, Talabat
Groceries)
• Direct API or data integration with supermarket POS or inventory systems
• Web application or desktop client
• Loyalty point integration with individual supermarket rewards programmes

6. Functional Requirements
6.1  Onboarding & Authentication
• [FR-01]  The app shall allow users to register and log in using a UAE mobile phone
number verified via SMS OTP (one-time passcode).
• [FR-02]  The app shall support Apple Sign-In on iOS and Google Sign-In on Android as
alternative authentication methods.
• [FR-03]  Users shall be able to browse a read-only version of the app as a guest (no
cart, no submissions) before registering.
• [FR-04]  The onboarding flow shall complete in no more than three screens and request
location permission with a plain-language explanation of why it is needed.
• [FR-05]  User profiles shall include: display name, profile photo (optional), preferred
language (Arabic/English), and home area (selected from a list of Dubai
neighbourhoods, not precise GPS).
• [FR-06]  During onboarding a tutorial of no more than three screens shall explain the
token economy and the data sourcing flow. The tutorial shall be skippable and re-
accessible from Settings.
• [FR-07]  First-time data sourcers shall receive a 50-token welcome bonus after their first
approved price submission, to incentivise the first contribution.

6.2  Map & Supermarket Discovery
• [FR-08]  The app shall display an interactive map (Google Maps SDK) showing all
participating supermarkets within the user's configured search radius (default: 3 km;
range: 500 m – 15 km).
• [FR-09]  Each supermarket pin on the map shall display: chain name, distance, a
freshness indicator showing how recently data was submitted (e.g. 'Updated 2 hrs ago'),
and a count of available price entries.
• [FR-10]  Tapping a supermarket pin shall open a store detail sheet listing all products
with submitted prices, sorted by most recently updated.
• [FR-11]  The map shall support filtering by supermarket chain. The following chains shall
be included at launch: Carrefour, Lulu Hypermarket, Spinneys, Waitrose, Union Coop,
Choithrams, Geant, Nesto, Al Maya, Grandiose.
• [FR-12]  The map shall visually distinguish supermarkets with 'stale' data (no submission
in the past 48 hours) using a greyed-out or flagged pin style.

6.3  Product & Price Discovery
• [FR-13]  Users shall be able to search for products by name, barcode scan, or category.
• [FR-14]  Product categories shall be pre-defined by admins and shall include at
minimum: Fresh Produce, Meat & Seafood, Dairy & Eggs, Bakery, Beverages,
Household, Baby & Kids, Health & Beauty, Frozen Foods, and Pantry.
• [FR-15]  Each product listing shall show: product name, pack size, brand, photo, the
lowest current price with the store name, and the time of the most recent submission.
• [FR-16]  Users shall be able to view a full price history chart for any product showing
price changes across all stores over the past 90 days.

• [FR-17]  Price submissions older than 72 hours shall be visually dimmed and labelled
'Unverified — may be outdated' until refreshed by a new submission.
• [FR-18]  Price submissions shall auto-expire and be removed from active results after 72

6.4  Grocery Cart
• [FR-19]  Registered users shall be able to add products to a Grocery Cart from any
product listing, search result, or store detail screen.
• [FR-20]  For each item in the cart, the app shall automatically surface the Best Available
Price: the lowest crowd-sourced price submitted within the user's configured radius in
the past 72 hours, along with the store name and distance.
• [FR-21]  The cart shall display a Total Basket Cost for each store option, showing what
the full basket would cost if purchased at a single store, to support consolidated
shopping decisions.
• [FR-22]  Users shall be able to adjust item quantities, remove items, and clear the cart.
• [FR-23]  The cart shall persist across sessions and devices linked to the same account.
• [FR-24]  When a better price becomes available for a cart item, the user shall receive an
in-app notification (not a push notification) on their next app open.

6.5  Item Request System
• [FR-25]  Users shall be able to submit a price data request for a specific product at a
specific supermarket branch.
• [FR-26]  Submitting a request shall cost the requester a configurable number of tokens
(default: 10 tokens per request; adjustable by admin).
• [FR-27]  Open requests shall be visible to all data sourcers on the Request Board,
sorted by token reward value and proximity to the sourcer's current or home location.
• [FR-28]  When a data sourcer fulfils a request, the requester shall be notified by push
notification and the tokens shall be transferred from the requester's balance to the
sourcer's balance.
• [FR-29]  If a request is not fulfilled within 48 hours, the tokens shall be refunded in full to
the requester.
• [FR-30]  Users shall be able to view their open and fulfilled request history in their
profile.

6.6  Data Sourcing & Price Submission
• [FR-31]  Any registered user may act as a data sourcer. There is no separate
registration; the role is unlocked by default.
• [FR-32]  To submit a price, a sourcer shall: select the supermarket, search for or scan
the product barcode, enter the price manually (AED), and optionally attach a photo of the
price label.
• [FR-33]  The app shall display a real-time token estimate on the submission form before
the sourcer commits, showing: base token reward, any active decay for duplicate nearby

Note: The decay formula shall be defined by the admin during configuration. Factors include: recency of

6.7  Token Economy & Voucher Rewards
• [FR-37]  Each user shall have a Token Balance displayed prominently on their profile
and home screen.
• [FR-38]  Tokens shall be earned by: submitting prices (base rate), receiving receipt
verification bonus (1.5x), fulfilling open requests (request reward), completing daily
challenges, and achieving streak milestones.
• [FR-39]  Tokens shall be spent by: submitting item requests (10 tokens per request,
configurable).
• [FR-40]  When a data sourcer's token balance reaches a configurable threshold (default:
500 tokens), the system shall automatically queue a voucher for approval. Once
approved by an admin, the voucher code shall be delivered via in-app notification and
email.
• [FR-41]  Vouchers shall be denomination-specific by supermarket (e.g., AED 50
Carrefour voucher) and linked to the store the sourcer submitted the most data for in the
qualifying period.
• [FR-42]  Token transactions shall be fully auditable; users shall be able to view a
complete token history showing each earn and spend event with timestamp, amount,
and reason.
• [FR-43]  Tokens shall have no cash value and are non-transferable between users.

6.8  Gamification
• [FR-44]  The app shall maintain a monthly Leaderboard showing the top 10 data
sourcers by tokens earned in the current calendar month. A user's own rank shall always
be visible even if outside the top 10.
• [FR-45]  The leaderboard shall update within 5 minutes of any new approved
submission. Previous month's top 3 shall be displayed in a Hall of Fame section.
• [FR-46]  The app shall implement a Streak system: consecutive days on which a sourcer
submits at least one approved price entry. Streak milestones (7 days, 30 days, 100
days) shall award bonus tokens and a visible badge.

• [FR-47]  The app shall surface one Daily Challenge per day for data sourcers (e.g.
'Submit 5 prices from Carrefour DIFC today'). Completing the challenge awards a bonus
token pool on top of the standard earn rate.
• [FR-48]  The app shall use a Level system (Levels 1–20) based on cumulative lifetime
tokens earned. Each level shall unlock a badge displayed on the user's profile and a
small permanent earn-rate multiplier (e.g. Level 5: +5% on all submissions).
• [FR-49]  The app shall issue Achievement Badges for non-routine milestones, including:
first submission, first verified submission, first request fulfilled, 100 total submissions,
and first month in the top 10 leaderboard.
• [FR-50]  Users shall receive a push notification when their leaderboard rank changes by
3 or more positions, and when they earn a new badge. Rank-change notifications shall
be opt-out.

6.9  Trust & Moderation
• [FR-51]  Any user shall be able to flag a price submission as: 'Price is wrong', 'Product
doesn't match', 'Store is wrong', or 'Duplicate entry'.
• [FR-52]  A submission flagged by 3 or more unique users shall be automatically hidden
from the active feed and queued for admin review. The original sourcer shall be notified.
• [FR-53]  The admin moderation dashboard shall display: a queue of flagged
submissions with flag count and reasons, the ability to remove or reinstate a submission,
warn a user, or permanently ban a user.
• [FR-54]  Sourcers who have 3 or more submissions removed within a rolling 30-day
period shall be automatically suspended for 7 days, with an in-app and email notification.
A third suspension within 90 days shall result in a permanent ban requiring manual
review.
• [FR-55]  Each sourcer shall have an Accuracy Rating (percentage of submissions not
flagged or removed over their lifetime) visible on their public profile.
• [FR-56]  Admins shall have the ability to edit a submission's price, product, or store
mapping to correct errors without removing the submission entirely.

6.10  Admin & Data Monitoring Dashboard
• [FR-57]  The admin dashboard shall be a web-based tool (not part of the mobile app)
accessible to authorised internal staff only.
• [FR-58]  The dashboard shall display real-time and historical views of: total submissions
per day/week/month; active users and data sourcers; tokens earned and spent;
vouchers issued and pending.
• [FR-59]  The dashboard shall include a Product Trends module showing: which products
have the highest search and request volumes (trending products), price movement
charts per product per store over selectable time ranges, and category-level demand
heatmaps by Dubai neighbourhood.
• [FR-60]  The dashboard shall provide a Sourcer Activity map view showing which areas
of Dubai have the most recent and dense data coverage vs. areas with stale or no data,
to guide community outreach.

7. User Stories
The following user stories were developed collaboratively by the product and stakeholder team.
Priority is indicated as Must Have (MH), Should Have (SH), or Could Have (CH). Stories
sourced from the initial discovery workshop are marked accordingly. Open questions raised
during review are captured in the notes column of the detailed story table below.

7.1  Onboarding & Profile
US-01  [Must Have]  As a new user, I want to sign up with my phone number or Apple/Google
login in under 60 seconds, so that I can start browsing prices immediately without a lengthy
registration process
US-02  [Should Have]  As a new user, I want to complete a brief 3-screen tutorial explaining
tokens and the leaderboard, so that I understand the app's core value proposition before I
engage with it

7.2  Price Submission (Data Sourcing)
US-03  [Must Have]  As a data sourcer, I want to post a grocery price with a photo, store, item
name, and price in AED, so that other users can find current prices and I earn tokens for
contributing
Note: Price and discount are entered manually, not via OCR. The submission may capture a percentage
discount, a fixed discount value, or a promotional offer type (BOGO, bundle). This must be clearly
communicated in the submission UI.

US-04  [Must Have]  As a data sourcer, I want to see how many tokens I will earn before I
submit a price, so that I understand the reward decay and act quickly to maximise my token
income
Note: The token estimate shown in real time must factor in: whether the same product+store combo has a
recent submission (decay), whether there is an open request that matches (bonus), and whether a receipt
is attached (1.5x multiplier). The decay formula parameters are admin-configurable.

US-05  [Should Have]  As a data sourcer, I want to attach my receipt to a price submission, so
that my entry passes verification and I receive the full 1.5x token multiplier
Note: Receipt verification in v1.0 is manual admin review rather than automated OCR. Admin view of
receipt queue is required.

US-06  [Should Have]  As a data sourcer, I want to see my daily submission limit clearly, so that
I understand the anti-spam cap before I reach it and plan my submissions accordingly
Note: A bypass mechanism for verified high-accuracy sourcers (admin-granted) should be considered for
v1.1. Elevated to Must Have priority for the daily cap display; bypass is a Should Have.

about drops within 3 km of me, so that I can act on a saving while the data is still fresh

8. Non-Functional Requirements
8.1  Performance
• [NFR-01]  The app shall launch (cold start) in under 3 seconds on a mid-range device

10. UI / UX Requirements
10.1  Design Principles
• Clarity over cleverness: price data must be immediately readable; no ambiguity about
which price belongs to which store.
• Trust-first: every price entry must display its age and verification status. Users should
never have to guess whether data is fresh.
• Rewarding contribution: the token earn screen and submission confirmation must feel
satisfying — use micro-animations and positive feedback to reinforce the behaviour.
• Speed: the most common journey (open app → build cart → see best prices) must
require no more than 3 taps from the home screen.

10.2  Navigation Structure
The app shall use a bottom tab bar with five items: Home / Feed, Map, Cart, Leaderboard &
Tokens, and Profile & Settings. No more than three levels of navigation depth for any task.

10.3  Key Screen Requirements
• [UI-01]  The home feed shall default to price entries sorted by recency, with a persistent
'Freshness Filter' chip row at the top (All / Under 6h / Under 24h / Under 72h).
• [UI-02]  The grocery cart shall show, for each item: item name, quantity stepper, the
best-price badge (store name + price in AED), and a secondary 'Also at' line showing the
next cheapest option.
• [UI-03]  The token balance widget on the home screen shall animate (count-up) when
new tokens are earned, providing immediate positive feedback to the sourcer.
• [UI-04]  The submission form shall show the live token estimate updating in real time as
the sourcer fills in fields (product, store, price, photo attachment).
• [UI-05]  All price values shall be displayed in AED with two decimal places (e.g. AED
14.75).

10.4  Branding & Design System
Final brand assets, colour tokens, typography scale, iconography, and component library are
maintained in the Figma workspace. All screens must be reviewed against the design system
before engineering handoff. Arabic typeface must be tested alongside Latin characters at every
type size.

13. Technology Stack Recommendation
This section documents the recommended technology stack and the rationale behind each
decision. The two primary constraints that drove these choices were: (1) true cross-platform
parity between iOS and Android with zero UI divergence, and (2) the need to handle both
transactional relational data and time-series price data efficiently within a single coherent
system.

13.1  Mobile Application — Flutter (Dart)
Recommendation: Flutter with Dart.

Flutter compiles to native ARM code on both iOS and Android from a single codebase,
rendering via its own Skia/Impeller graphics engine rather than wrapping native platform
widgets. This is the critical differentiator: because Flutter does not use platform-native UI
components, the pixel output on iOS and Android is identical by construction — addressing the
project's non-negotiable cross-platform parity requirement (NFR-24). React Native, by contrast,
renders through native components and requires platform-specific styling workarounds that
introduce visual and behavioural drift over time.

13.2  Backend API — Node.js with TypeScript
Recommendation: Node.js (TypeScript) with a RESTful + WebSocket architecture.

Node.js offers a non-blocking I/O model well-suited to the high-volume, low-latency nature of a
crowdsourced data feed. TypeScript adds static typing, reducing runtime errors in business-
critical areas like the token ledger and submission validation. The large Node.js ecosystem
provides mature libraries for every integration in Section 12 (Firebase Admin SDK, pg/Prisma
for PostgreSQL, ioredis for Redis, Socket.io for real-time feed updates). The team's existing
skills also align with this choice.

13.3  Primary Database — PostgreSQL with TimescaleDB
Recommendation: PostgreSQL (v16+) with the TimescaleDB extension.

PostgreSQL is the right foundation for this application because its data model is fundamentally
relational: users, stores, products, token transactions, and requests all have strong foreign-key
relationships that benefit from ACID guarantees. PostgreSQL's JSONB column type also
handles flexible product metadata (brand, pack size, nutritional info) without requiring a
separate document store.

TimescaleDB is a PostgreSQL extension — not a separate database — that adds automatic
time-series partitioning (hypertables) for append-heavy, time-stamped data. Price submissions
are exactly this pattern: millions of rows with a timestamp, product ID, store ID, and price.
TimescaleDB makes the 90-day price history charts (FR-16) and the admin trend dashboard
(FR-59) orders of magnitude faster than a vanilla PostgreSQL table scan, without introducing a
second database technology to operate and maintain.

13.4  Admin Monitoring Dashboard — Metabase
Recommendation: Metabase (self-hosted, open-source edition).

Metabase connects directly to PostgreSQL/TimescaleDB with a no-code query builder, making
it accessible to non-technical operations staff for day-to-day monitoring. Custom SQL
dashboards cover the product trend charts and sourcer coverage maps required in FR-57
through FR-62. Self-hosting keeps all operational data within the same Google Cloud
environment and avoids PII being transmitted to a third-party SaaS analytics tool.

15. Constraints & Assumptions
15.1  Constraints
• Apple App Store guidelines require Apple Sign-In whenever a third-party social login is
offered — this is mandatory for the iOS build.
• Google Play policies prohibit token or point systems that function as real-money
equivalents. Vouchers must be clearly framed as promotional rewards, not currency, in
all in-app copy and the Terms of Service.
• UAE PDPL requires explicit, informed consent for location data collection. The app must
not activate GPS without a clearly worded consent prompt that the user affirmatively
accepts.
• Voucher procurement agreements with supermarket chains (Carrefour, Lulu, etc.) must
be signed before launch; the token threshold cannot be activated without confirmed
voucher inventory.
• Price data accuracy is entirely dependent on community participation. At launch,
coverage will be sparse; the app must communicate data freshness and gaps clearly to
avoid misleading users.
• The app must support Arabic as a first-class language. All content — including user-
generated product names and store comments — must support Unicode Arabic
characters throughout the stack.

15.2  Assumptions
• A critical mass of data sourcers will be recruited and onboarded before public launch
through a targeted pre-launch campaign; the app is not assumed to be self-seeding from
day one.
• Google Cloud's me-central1 (Doha/UAE) region will remain available and price-stable for
the duration of the initial contract term.
• The token decay formula will be tuned iteratively post-launch based on observed sourcer
behaviour; the initial parameters are a starting estimate, not a fixed contract.
• Supermarket chains will not change their store layouts, branch lists, or branding
significantly within the first 12 months, allowing the pre-loaded store directory to remain
accurate.
• Users in Dubai have sufficient smartphone penetration and 4G connectivity for the app's
core flows to function reliably in all targeted neighbourhoods.
• The administrative team will have sufficient capacity to review the receipt verification
queue within 24 hours of submission; SLA targets for admin review will be defined in the
operational runbook.

16. Appendix
16.1  Glossary
Term Definition
Data Sourcer A registered user who physically visits supermarkets and submits
crowd-sourced price data in exchange for tokens.
Shopper A registered user who uses the app primarily to compare prices and
build grocery carts.
Token The in-app reward unit earned by data sourcers and spent by shoppers
making requests. Tokens have no cash value.
Voucher A supermarket-issued discount code awarded to data sourcers when
their token balance crosses the redemption threshold.
Decay Formula An admin-configurable algorithm that reduces the token reward for a
submission when a recent, similar submission already exists for the
same product and store.
Best Available Price The lowest crowd-sourced price for a product across all stores within
the user's configured radius, submitted within the past 72 hours.
Request Board A screen showing all open item price requests from shoppers, visible to
data sourcers and sorted by token reward value.
Freshness The age of a price submission. Submissions under 72 hours are 'fresh';
older ones are dimmed and labelled as potentially outdated.
Accuracy Rating A data sourcer's percentage of approved (not flagged or removed)
submissions over their lifetime, used as a trust indicator.
Streak Consecutive days of at least one approved price submission by a data
sourcer. Breaking the streak resets the counter.
AED UAE Dirham — the currency used for all prices displayed within the
app.
RTL Right-to-left text direction, required for Arabic language display.
TimescaleDB A PostgreSQL extension that adds hypertable partitioning for time-
series data, used for price history storage and trend queries.
Hypertable A TimescaleDB construct that automatically partitions a large time-
stamped table into chunks by time interval for query performance.
